# Principle 09: Retro After Every Sprint

## Principle

Every sprint ends with a retrospective document. What was built, what worked, what didn't, what bugs surfaced, what spec deviations occurred, what ADRs need to be written, and what carries forward to the next sprint.

The retro is the artifact, not the meeting.

## Why

Sprints accumulate learning that evaporates quickly if not captured. The retro turns that learning into input for the next planning cycle.

## Where this came from

An early sprint retro was brief and mostly descriptive. A later verification-focused retro was far more useful because it captured actual bugs, spec drift, and follow-up decisions. That document became the source for future planning, fresh ADRs, and explicit risk carry-forward.

## How to apply

Every sprint ends with a retro written while context is still fresh. It should answer:

- what was the sprint goal and whether it was achieved
- what was completed versus deferred
- what bugs were found, especially during verification
- what spec deviations occurred and why
- what new risks or tickets emerged
- what process changes should be adopted next time
